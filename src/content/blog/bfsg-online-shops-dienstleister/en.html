Why online shops and service providers are at the centre of the BFSG

Since 28 June 2025, Germany's Accessibility Strengthening Act (BFSG) has been in force. It is not a niche public-sector topic: at its core it targets companies that offer digital products and services to consumers. Online shops, appointment booking, customer accounts, banking and reservation flows are named explicitly – exactly the features most commercial websites earn their money with.

If you run a shop or sell, initiate or enable bookings for services online, you are very likely covered. An exemption applies only to micro-enterprises in the service sector – fewer than ten employees and at most €2M annual turnover. Pure B2B offerings without a consumer relationship are generally out of scope. Important: this is a technical assessment, not legal advice – if in doubt, clarify the scope with a specialist lawyer.

Which parts of your shop are actually affected

Accessibility doesn't end on the homepage. The BFSG is about the entire path a customer takes from the first page to the order confirmation. For a shop, these are the focus areas:

  • Product lists and filters: categories, sorting and filters must be keyboard-operable and understandable to screen readers – not driven by mouse and colour alone.
  • Product detail pages: images need meaningful alt text, and variant selectors (size, colour) must be correctly labelled.
  • Cart and checkout: every step must work without a mouse, required fields must be clearly marked, and error messages must be unambiguously tied to the field they concern.
  • Customer account and forms: registration, login and address forms are among the most common stumbling blocks.
  • Confirmations and invoices: confirmation pages, emails and PDF invoices fall under the requirements too.

The typical barriers in e-commerce

The same patterns recur in practice – and nearly all of them are fixable:

  • Errors signalled only in red: conveying "please complete this field" through colour alone helps no one with low vision. The message must exist as text and be linked to the field.
  • Keyboard traps in modals: cart layers or cookie banners you cannot escape with the keyboard.
  • Payment iframes: embedded payment fields (credit card, PayPal) must also be operable and labelled.
  • Status by colour only: "in stock" green, "sold out" red – imperceptible to some without text or an icon.
  • Contrast in sale designs: light-grey prices, thin discount badges and low-contrast buttons fail every audit.

WooCommerce, Shopify & co. – what it means for you

No shop system is "accessible out of the box". WooCommerce, Shopify and others give you a solid foundation, but actual compliance depends on your theme, your plugins and your content. Especially with heavily customised themes or many third-party plugins, most barriers emerge only from the combination. A standard theme is no free pass – what matters is what actually reaches the browser in the end.
